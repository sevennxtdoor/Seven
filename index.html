Features

One notch, ten pages.

Small, it shows whatever is happening right now. Rest your cursor on it and it widens into a panel; slide two fingers sideways on the touchpad to move between pages.

Music that wears its cover

Cover, title and a live visualizer from Spotify, YouTube in your browser, or any app in the Windows media controls. The panel turns to frosted glass in the cover's colors, and the text takes on its accent. Shuffle, skip and lyrics are one tap away.

Good manners

There when you look. Gone when you don't.

01

Quiet mode, your way

With nothing to show, a small light drifts slowly across a calm gradient, and settles into a slow pulse when you step away. Prefer the time and battery? Switch the light off in Settings.

02

A thin line in fullscreen

During a fullscreen video or game the notch shrinks to a slim line in your music's colors, and stays quiet.

03

Behind a maximized window it tucks away so your tabs stay clickable, then drops down for a moment when the volume changes or a message arrives.

Hold your cursor at the very top edge and it comes back. It moves on spring physics, smooth on 120 and 144 Hz screens.

Privacy & safety

What Seven does, and doesn't.

There's no account and no Seven server. Everything below is the full list.

It does

  • Install into your user folder, without admin rights, and uninstall cleanly from Settings → Apps.
  • Keep one local file, %AppData%\Seven\data.json, for to-dos, water, shelf links and settings. It's saved through a temporary file, so a crash can't empty it.
  • Show notifications after Windows asks your permission, only inside the notch.
  • Start with Windows only if you leave that box ticked in the installer.

It never

  • Reads your keys. Volume and brightness come from the Windows audio and display settings.
  • Saves your clipboard. Clipboard history is off by default, text only, in memory, and skips what password managers mark private.
  • Copies your files. The shelf only remembers where they are.
  • Sends anything about you except the three requests listed below.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Does it cost anything?
No. Use it at home or at work, on as many PCs as you like.
Coming from 1.0?
Run the new installer over it. Your to-dos, shelf, water count and settings stay.
Which players does it pick up?
Whatever appears in the Windows media flyout next to the volume: Spotify, YouTube and YouTube Music in Chrome, Edge, Brave or Firefox, and most modern players. Shuffle works where the app allows it.
Music on the lock screen?
Windows doesn't let apps draw there, for good security reasons. Seven's music card in the middle of the screen is the closest thing, and you can switch it off.
Is the clipboard feature private?
It's off until you turn it on. Then it holds the last 25 pieces of text in memory only, skips anything marked private by password managers, and forgets everything when Seven closes.
Why 52 MB?
The installer brings its own .NET runtime, so there's nothing else to install and it behaves the same on every PC.
How heavy is it?
Usually 90 to 130 MB in Task Manager, most of it the Windows graphics and media frameworks underneath. Seven hands memory back after you close the panel.
Where are the settings?
Right-click the notch → Settings, or swipe to the last page of the panel.
Why does it vanish behind big windows?
So your browser tabs stay clickable. It still comes down for volume, messages and timers, and holding the cursor at the top edge brings it back.
Desktop PC or several screens?
Both work. Brightness needs a laptop panel, since most external monitors don't let Windows change it, and the notch lives on your main display.
How do I turn it off?
Right-click the notch → Quit Seven. To remove it: Settings → Apps → Installed apps → Seven → Uninstall.
Found something odd?
Tell me in a GitHub issue. Bugs and ideas are both welcome.
